# Post Structure

A hook earns the click. The structure decides whether they finish.

**The default shape**, if you remember nothing else:

```
1. HOOK              1 line     the claim, the number, the quote
2. PAYOFF            1 line     answer what line 1 raised. Immediately.
3. CONTEXT           1-2 lines  only what's needed to understand the rest
4. THE MEAT          3-8 lines  the story, the steps, the data
5. THE LESSON        1-2 lines  what a stranger should take away
6. CLOSE             1 line     a specific question, or nothing at all
```

Six parts. Under 1,400 characters. Under 15 lines. That shape alone beats most of
what is on the platform.

**Length targets, from the measured data:**

| | Target | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 43–78 characters | 114 |
| Whole post | under 1,400 characters | 2,900 |
| Lines | under 15 | 38 |

Posts under ~1,400 characters roughly **doubled** the median reach of posts over
~3,000. If your draft is long, the question is not "how do I trim this" — it is
"is this two posts?"

## The 15 shapes

Each one: when to use it, the skeleton, and how it breaks.

---

### F1 — The Playbook
**The workhorse. Use it most.**

Someone can act on this tomorrow. Highest bookmark and share rate of any format,
which makes it your best lead generator even though it will lose the reach game.

```
L1: receipt hook ending in a colon
L2: scope qualifier — "For agencies under 10 people." or a negation stack
BODY: 3–6 bold section headers
      under each: 2–4 lines, each self-contained (claim + reason or number)
      where relevant, name the manual route AND the shortcut
optional: "What NOT to do:" near the end
CLOSE: one specific question
```
- **Length:** 1,200–2,400 characters. This is the one format allowed to run long.
- **Media:** carousel or a single dense diagram.
- **Breaks when:** the steps are generic advice. Every line must contain something
  only you would know. If a competitor could have written the line, cut it.

---

### F2 — The Lesson Dump

```
L1: "[N] things I learned [doing specific hard thing]:"
BODY: 8–20 one-line lessons, verb-first, each with its own micro-detail
CLOSE: question, or "which one did I get wrong?"
```
- **Length:** 900–1,800 characters.
- **Media:** carousel, one lesson per slide.
- **Breaks when:** the lessons are one-liners with nothing under them. Fewer points
  with a sub-line each beats twenty one-liners nobody can act on.
- **Reusable:** re-ship it bigger later — 9 lessons becomes 15 lessons, same hook.

---

### F3 — The Transparency Report

Your own numbers, including the bad ones.

```
L1: the headline number, good or bad
BODY: → line per channel/project/month, with real figures
      include at least one number that does not flatter you
      name what failed AND what you changed
CLOSE: a self-audit line that pre-empts the cynic
```
- **Length:** 800–1,600 characters.
- **Media:** a screenshot of the actual dashboard, cropped. Real beats designed here.
- **Breaks when:** every number is good. Then it is a brag with a chart. The
  negative number is the entire reason this format works.
- **Check `02-INTAKE.md` first.** Some numbers are internal-only forever.

---

### F4 — The Confession / Arc

```
L1: the confession, stated flatly
L2: the stakes, in numbers
BODY: one beat per line. Short lines. Tense can flip to present at the worst moment.
      Be honest if it did not end well.
CLOSE: the recovery, or the aphorism. No CTA.
```
- **Length:** 900–1,800 characters.
- **Media:** one real photo. Not a stock image, not an illustration.
- **Breaks when:** there is no recovery and no lesson. Pure vulnerability with no
  takeaway broke out **zero times** in the measured data. The confession is the
  setup, not the post.

---

### F5 — The Encounter Story

```
L1: a real quote, cold. No setup.
L2: your reaction. One or two words is fine. "Fair question."
BODY: the exchange, escalating, verbatim-messy. Keep the filler words.
      Then the diagnosis: what was really going on.
CLOSE: the generalised lesson.
```
- **Length:** 600–1,400 characters.
- **Media:** optional. Often stronger with none.
- **Breaks when:** the dialogue is polished. Real speech is ungrammatical; that is
  what makes it read as true.
- **Rule:** prospects stay anonymous archetypes ("a Series A founder"). Clients get
  named **only** if they are on the approved list, and then name them *with* their result.

---

### F6 — The Myth Autopsy

```
L1: name the myth or the enemy
L2: concede something real — "Look, it's not terrible advice."
BODY: an analogy does the arguing. Physical and domestic, never business jargon.
      Then: "Here's what actually happens:"
CLOSE: aphorism.
```
- **Length:** 700–1,500 characters.
- **Media:** a simple diagram of the wrong model vs the right one.
- **Breaks when:** you argue with a strawman. Steelman it first or the comments will.

---

### F7 — Receipts, Then Take

For reacting to news or an industry moment.

```
L1: the event + "Here's what it actually means:"
BODY: neutral facts first, no spin
      then the pivot line, alone on its own row — "All wrong."
      then "Here's what actually matters:"
CLOSE: the application to your reader, then an aphorism.
```
- **Length:** 700–1,500 characters.
- **Media:** a screenshot of the thing you are reacting to.
- **Breaks when:** you post it three days late. This format has a 48-hour shelf life.

---

### F8 — The Case Study

```
L1: "Meet [Name] at [Company] — [one-line descriptor]."
BODY: **The problem:** 2–4 bullets
      **What we did:** 2–4 bullets, with the decisions, not the deliverables
      **What happened:** the number
      then an italic client quote, real and short
CLOSE: an offer, or an invitation to argue with the result.
```
- **Length:** 900–1,800 characters.
- **Media:** before/after. This format is nothing without it.
- **Breaks when:** it lists deliverables instead of decisions. Nobody cares that you
  made 41 screens. They care which three you argued about.
- **Serialise it.** "Proof #4" builds an expectation. Expectation builds return readers.

---

### F9 — The Launch

```
L1: the news, with the biggest number in it
L2: the contrast — what it was before vs now
BODY: → deliverables, or what changed
      then early proof if you have any
CLOSE: comment-gate with the trigger word in quotes, or a direct link in comment 1.
```
- **Length:** 600–1,400 characters.
- **Media:** the thing itself. Video if it moves.
- **Breaks when:** it opens with excitement instead of the thing. "Excited to
  announce" is the single most-scrolled opening on the platform.
- **Budget:** one big-launch treatment per quarter. More and they stop being events.

---

### F10 — The Dilemma

Genuinely ask for help. Do not fake it.

```
L1: "[Audience] — I need some help with something."
BODY: show the real artifact, raw. The actual pricing question, the actual two options.
      argue both sides honestly. Give the strongest version of each.
CLOSE: "Genuine question — what would you do?"
```
- **Length:** 600–1,200 characters.
- **Media:** a screenshot of the real thing, lightly redacted.
- **Breaks when:** you have already decided. People can tell, and the comments dry up.
- **Bonus:** the comments become your next post.

---

### F11 — The Comment-Gated Lead Magnet
**Highest engagement rate of any format in the measured data.** Use quarterly, not weekly.

```
L1: the effort receipt — "I spent 40 hours on this so you don't have to."
L2: the pain, mind-read in one line
BODY: what's inside, 3–5 lines
      the anti-gate promise: "No email. No newsletter signup. No DM funnel."
CLOSE: Comment "[exact short word]" and I'll send it.
       Then the friction remover: "No need to connect — I'll reply to your comment."
```
- **Length:** 700–1,300 characters.
- **Media:** the cover of the asset, or a preview page.
- **Breaks when:** the asset is thin. One disappointed download costs more trust
  than the post earned.
- **Quarterly.** Do this monthly and you become the person who gates things.

---

### F12 — The Curation Digest

```
L1: "The [N] best [things] I've seen this [period]:"
BODY: per item — bold name, one line on why it works, one line on how to copy it
CLOSE: "What did I miss?"
```
- **Length:** 800–1,600 characters.
- **Media:** carousel. **One item per slide.** This is the highest-value format a
  visual business has and most agencies never run it.
- **Breaks when:** you curate without a point of view. The selection *is* the work —
  say why each one made the list and what it beat.
- **Credit the source in line one.** "Steal" is the right verb: "Go steal this."

---

### F13 — The Craft Drop
**One line, plus the work. Nothing else.**

```
L1: what it is. Five to ten words.
(no body, no CTA, no context)
```
- **Length:** under 100 characters.
- **Media:** the work. Video if it moves, image if it does not.
- **Breaks when:** you add explanation. The whole power of this format is the
  confidence of saying nothing. If the work needs a paragraph, it is a case study.
- **Frequency:** this can be 30–40% of a design agency's feed. It is the cheapest
  post to make and it is what people follow a design agency for.

---

### F14 — The Hire / People Story

```
L1: NOT the hire. The gap. "I ran this company for two years with nobody to
    check my work."
L2: "That changed this week."
BODY: who they are, what they've done, what it unlocks
CLOSE: an emotional landing is allowed here. "Let's get to work, [Name]."
```
- **Length:** 500–1,200 characters.
- **Media:** a real photo of the person.
- **Breaks when:** it opens as a job ad. Job-ad-shaped posts had among the worst
  engagement in the design agency data measured for this pack.

---

### F15 — The Monthly Experiment

Roughly once a month, ship one post that fits none of the above. A single sentence
repeated. A screenplay. A list of 40 imperatives. A joke with no explanation.

**This slot is a feature, not an indulgence.** A month of posts that all fit a
template reads as machine-made, and the audience feels it before they can name it.

- **Rule:** never repeat an experiment. Invent an adjacent one.

---

## Endings

Ranked by how often they work, from the measured account:

1. **One specific question.** Names the variable. "Which of these would you attack
   first?" Never "Thoughts?"
2. **An aphorism, no CTA.** "The messy middle is the whole game." Increasingly the
   strongest close — it trusts the reader.
3. **A soft, throwaway plug.** Lowercase, one line, never a paragraph. "oh and if
   you need this done properly, you know where I am"
4. **The comment gate.** Only with a real asset behind it.
5. **A callback.** The hook returns with a twist.
6. **An imperative cascade.** "Charge more. Say no faster. Ship on the date."
7. **Nothing.** Ending on the last line of the story is allowed and often best.

**Never:** "Thoughts?" · "Agree?" · "Repost if you found this useful" · "Tag someone
who needs to see this" · more than one question · a link in the body.

**One question maximum.** Two questions get zero answers.
