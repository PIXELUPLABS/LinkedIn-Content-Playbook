# Writing Style

The hook gets the click. The style is why they finish and come back.

---

## 1. The hard limits

**2,900 characters.** LinkedIn's limit is 3,000, but the real ceiling is lower,
because **bold and italic letters on LinkedIn are not formatting.** They are
separate unicode characters, and the platform counts each one as **two**. A post
that looks like 2,600 characters can be rejected at 3,100.

Use 2,900 as the working ceiling. And remember the data says your target is under
1,400 anyway — the limit is a guardrail, not a goal.

**One sentence per line.** Then a blank line. Long paragraphs die on mobile, where
almost everyone reads you.

**Under 15 lines.** Posts over 38 lines got the worst reach in the measured set.

**No links in the body.** It suppresses reach badly. Link goes in the first comment,
every time, no exceptions.

**No hashtags.** They do nothing on LinkedIn now except make you look like 2019.

---

## 2. Be specific or say nothing

This is the single biggest difference between content that builds trust and content
that fills a slot.

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| a large project | a $40,000 project |
| recently | last Tuesday |
| significantly faster | from four months to three weeks |
| a client | a Series B fintech |
| a lot of startups | 61 discovery calls this year |
| it improved conversion | demo requests went from 6 a week to 19 |
| we work with great companies | [named client], [named client] |

**Never round.** 617 is believable. 600 is a guess. 61 calls reads as counted;
"about 60" reads as invented. The odd number *is* the proof.

**Real names, real numbers, real screenshots.** Precision is what makes a claim
believable. Everything else is adjectives.

---

## 3. Numbers

- **Lead with the number when you have one.** It goes in the hook.
- **Never invent one.** If you need a figure you do not have, write `[NEED: x]`
  and leave it. A plausible fake number is the most dangerous thing in this file.
- **Flex the metric, not yourself.** The number can be loud. You should not be.
  "We did $100k" plus three lines of how it felt is bragging. "We did $100k. I took
  home under $1,000." is a story.
- **Undersell the outcome.** Realistic numbers convert better than best-case ones,
  because your reader has been lied to by everyone else in their feed.
- **Disclose your bias before someone accuses you of it.** "I sell this, so
  obviously I'd say it" buys more trust than pretending to be neutral.

---

## 4. Banned words and phrases

**Openers that cost you reach:**
excited to announce · thrilled to share · humbled and honoured · delighted to ·
in today's fast-paced world · let that sink in

**Filler adjectives with no meaning:**
seamless · robust · cutting-edge · innovative · game-changing · world-class ·
best-in-class · leverage (as a verb) · synergy · holistic · bespoke · curated
(unless you literally curated) · resonates · impressive · fancy · knack · keen

**Engagement bait:**
Thoughts? · Agree? · Repost if… · Tag someone who… · Comment "yes" if… ·
Who else feels this? · Drop a 🙌 if…

**Hedges that kill a claim:**
in my humble opinion · I could be wrong but · sort of · kind of · arguably ·
it could be argued · generally speaking · most of the time

**AI tells** — cut on sight, they mark a post as machine-written:
delve · tapestry · realm · testament to · navigate the landscape · in the ever-
evolving world of · it's not just X, it's Y (as a repeated structure) ·
unlock the power of · elevate your · at the end of the day

> Add your own list to `02-INTAKE.md` §6. The words *you* would never say are more
> useful than any general list.

## 5. The moves that make it sound like a person

**The parenthetical aside.** A short, honest interruption in brackets. It is the
easiest way to sound like a human being rather than a brand account.

> "We shipped it in 11 days (which we will not be repeating)."
> "Their old site had 17 fonts. (I counted.)"
> "This is a 3D render — we're still decorating."

Use it for: the honest caveat, the joke, the number source, the self-deprecation.
One or two per post. Three is a tic.

**The one-word line.** After a quote or a claim, drop a single word on its own line.
"Fair question." / "Dude." / "It wasn't." It creates a beat, and beats are what
make writing feel spoken.

**The tense flip.** In a story, switch to present tense at the worst moment. "I walk
into the meeting and I completely bomb it." It puts the reader in the room.

**The concession.** Give the other side something real before you argue. "Look, it's
not terrible advice." A reader who feels understood will let you disagree with them.

**The physical analogy.** Explain a business idea with something domestic — cooking,
cars, houses, weather. Never explain business with more business.

**The self-audit line.** Pre-empt the cynic. "We're not outliers, and I'm not
claiming this works for everyone." Costs one line, buys the whole post.

## 6. Register — pick one per post

**Analytical.** Numbers, structure, calm. Wins engagement rate and replies.
Best for playbooks and teardowns.

**Humorous.** Wins reach — a humorous register ran ~1.6x the average breakout rate
in the measured data, and meme-format posts ~1.7x. Badly under-used by most
professional accounts.

**Direct.** Short, blunt, declarative. Best for contrarian posts and positions.

**Warm.** For people stories and hires. Use sparingly — pure vulnerability with no
lesson attached broke out zero times in the measured data.

**Do not mix two registers in one post.** A joke inside a serious teardown reads as
nerves. Pick the register in the first line and hold it.

---

## 7. Formatting

- **Bold unicode** is fine for section headers in long posts. Remember it costs
  double characters. Never bold a whole sentence.
- **Emoji:** at most one or two, and only as bullets or a single accent. Never in
  the hook. Never a row of them.
- **Arrows (`→`) beat bullet dots** for scannable lists. Dashes are fine too.
- **A single blank line** between thoughts. Two blank lines is a scroll gap.
- **Quotation marks, not asterisks**, for quoting speech.
- **Numbers as digits**, always. "3 weeks" not "three weeks". Digits stop the eye.

---

## 8. The read-aloud test

Read the draft out loud before you post. Every time. It catches, in about 40 seconds:

- Sentences you would never actually say
- Missing beats where you naturally paused
- Any line that made you cringe — cut it, your instinct was right
- Whether the hook still works when you cannot see it

If you run out of breath in a sentence, it is too long. If you speed up while
reading a section, that section is filler.

---

## 9. Writing as someone else

When you ghostwrite for a founder or a teammate, the rule is narrow and absolute:

**Voice is learned from their real posts. It is never invented.**

Before writing as someone, read 20 of their actual posts and note:
their sentence length, whether they use "I" or "we", their three most-used words,
how they open, how they close, whether they swear, whether they use numbers.

**When asked to rewrite something "in X's style," change only the voice.** Do not
add facts, do not add receipts, do not add length. A voice rewrite that arrives 40%
longer with three new claims is not a rewrite, it is a different post.

**The impostor test.** Before you ship anything written for someone else: would a
person who reads them every week notice this was not them? If you are not sure, it
fails. Send it to them with the two lines you are least sure about flagged.
