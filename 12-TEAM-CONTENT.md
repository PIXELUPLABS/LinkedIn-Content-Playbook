# Team-Led Content

Getting one founder to post is a content problem. Getting a team to post is an
**operations problem**, and it is why almost every attempt at this dies in month two.

---

## Why it usually fails

Someone decides "the whole team should be posting." A channel is created. Two people
post for three weeks. Then a deadline arrives, and it quietly stops.

Three reasons, every time:

**1. Nobody defined the process.** "Post more" is not a process. It is a wish. The
team has no idea what to post, how often, or what good looks like, so it competes
with real work and loses.

**2. It costs the team time they do not have.** If your system needs an hour a week
from a busy designer, your system will not run. Anything above 15 minutes a week per
person needs to be someone else's job.

**3. Nobody said what it was for.** People will not do something indefinitely without
knowing whether it worked.

> Worth knowing: when a growth lead went looking for an agency to run team-led content
> for a whole company, **not one of the agencies that pitched described their process.**
> They all described their output. That gap is why so few teams have this working —
> and why having a written process is itself a competitive advantage.

---

## The two models

Pick one. Do not try to run both.

### Model A — The capture call
**Best when: the team has real expertise and some willingness.**

- **15 minutes per person, per week.** One call, three questions from
  `09-QUESTION-BANK.md` Section B.
- Someone else records, transcribes, drafts and schedules.
- The team member's only jobs: talk for 15 minutes, and approve a draft.
- Output: 2–3 posts per person per week.

**Total cost to the team member: 20 minutes a week.** That is the number you have to
be able to say out loud, and it is the number that makes people say yes.

### Model B — Repurpose the winner
**Best when: the team is genuinely too busy, or reluctant.**

- The founder's account is already producing content that works.
- Take a winning post, rewrite it from a different person's angle, in their voice.
- They approve or reject. That is their entire involvement.
- Output: 2–3 posts per person per week, at close to zero cost to them.

**Rules for Model B, or it goes wrong fast:**
- The angle must genuinely be theirs. A designer's version of a positioning post is
  about the work, not the strategy. Same idea, different vantage point.
- **Never post as someone without them reading it.** Not once. The first time a
  teammate finds out what "they" said by reading it in the feed, the programme is over.
- Vary the timing. Five near-identical posts on one Tuesday reads as a campaign,
  because it is one.

**Most teams should start with Model B and graduate the willing ones to Model A.**

## Who posts what

Different roles have different natural material. Assigning by role is what stops
five people posting the same thing.

| Role | Their natural content | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder** | Positions, contrarian takes, business lessons, proof, hiring | 4x/week |
| **Designers / makers** | Craft drops, process, single decisions, rejected work | 2–3x/week |
| **Project / account leads** | Client patterns, how work actually runs, expectation management | 2x/week |
| **Engineers** | Technical constraints, build reality, what breaks | 1–2x/week |
| **Strategy / research** | Market patterns, what buyers actually compare | 1–2x/week |
| **New joiners** | First-90-days observations — genuinely high performing | 1x/week for 3 months |
| **Company page** | Proof, offers, announcements, testimonials. Bottom of funnel only. | 2–3x/week |

**The founder is the only person who can tell the story people attach to.** A team
can produce excellent top-of-funnel content. It cannot produce the narrative. Do not
ask it to, and do not judge it for failing to.

**The bottleneck of a founder-fronted plan is the founder.** Design around the time
they actually have, not the time you wish they had, or it stalls in week three.

## The approval flow

Keep it to three states. More states means drafts die in a queue.

```
DRAFT  →  APPROVED  →  SCHEDULED
```

- **Drafts land in one place.** One doc, one board, one channel. Not DMs.
- **Approval is a yes, a no, or one named change.** Not line edits. If someone is
  rewriting drafts, the voice work was not done — go back to `07-WRITING-STYLE.md` §9.
- **48-hour rule.** No response in 48 hours means it ships or it dies, decided in
  advance. Nothing rots in a queue.
- **The founder does not approve other people's posts.** That is a bottleneck with no
  upside. They approve their own, and set the rules for everyone else's.

---

## Amplification — the rule most teams get backwards

When a post matters, **everyone comments on the original.**

Do not have five people each publish their own version of the same message. You get
five weak signals instead of one strong one, and you compete against yourself in the
same feed on the same day.

- One post gets the push.
- Everyone else comments with a *real sentence* — a different angle, a related
  example, a genuine disagreement.
- **Never "Great post!"** It is visible, it is obviously coordinated, and it makes
  the whole team look like a bot farm.
- Comment within the first hour. That is when it matters most.

## Making it stick

**Give it an owner.** Not "the team." One named person whose job includes this. If
nobody owns it, it does not happen.

**Show the numbers monthly.** People keep doing things that visibly work. Share the
three lead signals from `11-MEASUREMENT.md`, not just impressions — impressions
demoralise everyone except the top performer.

**Celebrate the right thing.** When a post produces a real conversation with a buyer,
say so publicly inside the company. That is the behaviour you want repeated, and it
is invisible unless someone announces it.

**Make it optional but attractive.** Mandatory posting produces mandatory-sounding
posts. Make it easy, make the results visible, and let people opt in.

**Run a paid incentive once.** A small bounty for the best asset, the best hook, or
the best first post gets people over the initial hump far more cheaply than months of
reminding.

---

## What to do in the first 30 days

**Week 1.** Pick the model. Name the owner. Fix the founder's profile
(`13-PROFILE-FOUNDATION.md`). Set up the asset folder (`08-MEDIA-AND-IMAGES.md` §6).

**Week 2.** Run one capture call with the two most willing people. Draft from it.
Show them the drafts. Getting two people a good first post is worth more than
briefing ten.

**Week 3.** Those two post. Everyone else sees what it looks like and what it cost
them. This is the recruitment step, and it works far better than a kickoff meeting.

**Week 4.** Add two more people. Set the cadence. Book the recurring calls.

**Do not launch this to the whole company on day one.** A programme that starts with
everyone and shrinks looks like a failure. One that starts with two and grows looks
like something people want to be part of.
