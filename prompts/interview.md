# Extract insights from a transcript

Here is a transcript. Mine it.

```
[PASTE TRANSCRIPT]
```

**The bar — read this before you start.**

> A story is not an insight. It is the **proof** of one.

Your job is to find the transferable **rules** and attach the stories as evidence.
An anecdote with no rule inside it is not usable. A rule with no evidence is a platitude.

**For every insight you find, give me all four parts:**

```
RULE:      one sentence a stranger could act on tomorrow
MECHANISM: why it's true — the actual reason, not a restatement
RECEIPT:   the verbatim quote, the number, or the named example from the transcript
BREAKS:    the conditions where this rule stops holding
```

**"BREAKS" is not optional.** Anyone can state a rule. Knowing where it fails is the
expertise, and it is what makes the resulting content credible instead of generic.

**Also extract, separately:**

1. **Quotable lines** — anything said in a way better than I would write it. Verbatim,
   with the speaker. These become hooks.
2. **Numbers** — every figure mentioned, with what it refers to and how confident the
   speaker sounded.
3. **Named examples** — companies, clients, people. Flag any that might be confidential.
4. **Open loops** — anything said that you did not fully understand, or that was
   started and not finished. These become next session's questions.

**Then:**

Rank the insights by how *specific* and *non-obvious* they are. Tell me the top three
and, for each one, which format from `06-POST-STRUCTURE.md` it should become and why.

**Do not include:** anything personal, anything about pay or pricing that was not
clearly public, anything said about a named third party that would embarrass them,
or anything the speaker flagged as off the record.
