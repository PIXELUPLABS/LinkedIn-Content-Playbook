# Profile Foundation

**The cheapest win in this pack.** Content sends people to your profile. If the
profile does not convert them, every post is paying for someone else's traffic.

This is a one-time job. Two hours. Do it before you write another post.

---

## The path a real buyer takes

```
sees a post  →  clicks the name  →  scans the headline  →  reads 2 lines of the About
             →  looks at the banner  →  checks Featured  →  clicks a link  →  or leaves
```

**Every one of those steps is a place to lose them.** Most profiles lose people at
the headline and never find out.

---

## 1. The headline

Not your job title. **What you do for whom, and the proof.**

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Founder @ [Company] | Brand + product design for B2B companies selling into enterprise |
| Designer \| Creative \| Entrepreneur | We design the site your buyer checks before they reply |
| Helping brands tell their story | Design for companies moving upmarket · 40+ launches |

Rules:
- Say who it is for. A named audience beats a clever line.
- Include one number if you have one.
- **Remove any word you would not say out loud.** "Passionate," "storyteller,"
  "visionary."
- It gets truncated on mobile — front-load the important half.

---

## 2. The About section

Only the **first two lines** are visible before "…see more". Treat those two lines
exactly like a post hook.

```
Line 1-2   THE HOOK          who you help and what changes. This is all most people read.
Then       THE PROBLEM       what is broken for them, in their words
Then       WHAT YOU DO       specifically. Named services, not adjectives.
Then       THE PROOF         clients, numbers, outcomes — only what §3 of 02-INTAKE.md allows
Then       WHO IT IS NOT FOR one line. It builds more trust than anything above it.
Last       THE NEXT STEP     one link, with a UTM tag
```

Write it in first person. A third-person bio on a personal profile reads as a press
release.

---

## 3. The Featured section

**The most under-used real estate on the platform**, and the one thing a curious
buyer will actually click.

Four slots, in this order:

1. **Your best proof.** The case study or piece of work that closes people.
2. **Your best utility post.** The one that gets bookmarked. It proves you are worth
   following as well as hiring.
3. **A lead magnet or the audit/offer.** Something with a low-commitment yes.
4. **The way to contact you.** A calendar link or a form. Tagged.

Give each one a real title and a real thumbnail. A default LinkedIn preview looks
like nothing and gets clicked like nothing.

**Refresh it quarterly.** A featured post from 18 months ago says the account is dormant.

---

## 4. The banner

Most profiles waste it on a stock gradient or a logo. It is the largest visual
element on the page, and for a design business it is a live audition.

It should carry, at a glance:
- What you do, in a few words
- One proof point — a number, or three recognisable client logos
- Nothing that gets covered by the profile photo (bottom-left is dead space)

**Design it for mobile.** Most people see it cropped much tighter than the desktop
preview suggests. Check it on a phone before you commit.

> If you run a design or creative business, this is the highest-stakes graphic you
> own. A weak banner on a design agency's profile is an argument against hiring you,
> made before anyone reads a word.

---

## 5. Links and tagging

**Every outbound link gets a UTM tag.** Otherwise all of it lands in your analytics
as "direct" and you will conclude LinkedIn does nothing.

- The link in the bio / contact info
- Every link in the Featured section
- The company page website link
- Every link you put in a first comment

Use a consistent naming scheme so the data is readable six months from now:
`?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=profile&utm_campaign=bio`

---

## 6. The company page

People check it after they read the founder. It converts more of them than it reaches.

- Tagline that says what you do, not what you believe
- A current banner
- Real "About" copy, not the boilerplate from your incorporation documents
- Recent activity — an abandoned page reads as an abandoned company
- Team members with the page correctly listed as their employer, so their profiles
  link back to it

---

## The 2-hour checklist

- [ ] Headline says who it is for and includes a number
- [ ] About: first two lines work as a hook on their own
- [ ] About: includes one "not for you if…" line
- [ ] Featured: four current items, real titles, real thumbnails
- [ ] Banner: readable on a phone, carries one proof point
- [ ] Profile photo: recent, close-cropped, plain background
- [ ] Every link tagged with UTMs
- [ ] Company page: current banner, real about copy, recent posts
- [ ] Team members list the company correctly
- [ ] Creator mode / relevant settings on, so people can follow without connecting
- [ ] Custom profile URL claimed

---

## Do this before the content, not after

A profile fix is one afternoon and it improves the return on **every post you will
ever publish**. Content with a broken profile is a leaking bucket you keep pouring into.
