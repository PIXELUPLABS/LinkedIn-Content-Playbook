# The Operating System

How the machine runs when nobody is feeling inspired.

---

## 1. How often to post

**Four times a week. Not seven.**

This is the clearest finding in the measured data and the most counter-intuitive.

| Posts that week | What happened to the week's TOTAL reach |
|---|---|
| 4 posts | Highest total reach of any group |
| 5 posts | ~10% lower |
| 6 posts | ~27% lower |
| 7 posts | **Under half of a 4-post week** |

Read that again. It is not that the fifth post gets less reach. It is that the
whole week gets less reach. The fifth, sixth and seventh posts appear to cost more
than they add.

**Confidence: medium.** The sample was small, and there is a plausible other
explanation: busy weeks might be launch weeks, when the quality bar drops. So
treat this as a strong prompt to run your own test, not as a law. But if you are
currently posting daily and the numbers are flat, this is the first thing to change.

**Practical rule: one post a day, maximum.** More splits attention. Fewer than
three a week and you lose the habit, which is the real killer.

---

## 2. The weekly mix

Decide the mix before the week starts, not post by post.

A good default for a business that wants leads:

| Slot | Type | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 2 per week | **Utility** — a hack, a playbook, a teardown, a checklist | Gets you bookmarked, shared, and tagged. This is the lead engine. |
| 1 per week | **Position** — a contrarian take, a myth you are killing | Makes you memorable and gives people a reason to argue in your comments |
| 1 per week | **Proof** — a client outcome, a piece of work, a real result | Converts the people the other three posts warmed up |
| ~1 per month | **Experiment** — something that fits none of the above | Stops the account reading like a machine |

**Why the mix matters.** Reach with nothing to convert on is wasted attention.
Conversion posts with no reach are shouting in an empty room. Most accounts fail
by doing only one of the two for months.

---

## 3. Where posts come from

**Never start from a blank page.** Not for yourself, not for a teammate. Blank-page
ideation on the morning you need to post produces your worst work, every time.

Four sources, in order of quality:

**1. The question bank.** Open `09-QUESTION-BANK.md`, pick a question, answer it
out loud into a voice memo. This is the highest-yield source and it never runs dry.

**2. Real questions from sales calls.** The questions prospects actually ask are
the questions the market is actually asking. If three people asked it this month,
it is a post. Keep a running list.

**3. Capture in the wild.** A post that annoyed you. A tweet you agreed with. A
line from a client call. Save it *in the moment* to one place — a note, a channel,
a doc. Ideas do not survive until Friday.

**4. Your own winners.** A post that worked is raw material for three more: a
longer version, a shorter version, and the opposite argument.

### The two content types — and the one that matters

- **Stories** are tied to a moment. They are told once and they expire.
- **Insights** are true whenever you post them. They never expire.

**Save every insight to a library. Let stories go.** Insights get reused,
reframed and re-shipped for years. This one filing habit is the difference
between an account that gets harder to run over time and one that gets easier.

### A post that worked will not work again on the same audience

Its value was that it was new. That is spent. You can re-ship the *idea* in a
different format, from a different angle, with different proof. You cannot re-ship
the post.

---

## 4. The 90-day rule

**Do not repeat an angle within 90 days on the same channel.**

Same theme is fine — you should have a small number of themes and hit them
repeatedly. Same *angle* is not. If you argued "design is a sales problem" from
the enterprise-buyer angle in March, argue it from the founder-time angle in May.

Check scheduled posts too, not just published ones. A draft that repeats something
already queued for next Tuesday will ship second and look like a rerun.

---

## 5. THE GATE

Every draft passes all six checks before it goes out. Including drafts the founder
wrote. Especially those.

**1. One argument.**
The post makes exactly one claim. If it makes two, it is two posts. Split it.

**2. A position, not a topic.**
It starts from something you actually believe (see `02-INTAKE.md` §5), not from
"a thing people are talking about." If no position fits, you do not have a view
yet, and the draft waits until you do.

**3. A receipt.**
At least one real specific: a number, a named story, a real quote, a screenshot.
Grounded in something you actually did.

> **Exception that matters:** do not police the founder's own numbers. If they
> said "we basically doubled it" or "a few weeks back" or "3x", that is voice, not
> error. Humans are vague and it reads as human. Never quietly swap their loose
> number for a precise one they did not use.
>
> But invent nothing. A number with no origin at all is a fabrication, and that is
> a different thing from a number spoken loosely.

**4. In voice.**
Sounds like the person whose name is on it. Voice is learned from their real
posts, never invented. If you cannot hear them saying it, rewrite it.

**5. Fresh angle.**
Not a repeat of anything from the last 90 days, published or scheduled.

**6. No landmines.**
This is the only check that is strict, because these are promises a customer can
hold you to:
- No price, package or guarantee that has not publicly launched
- No client name that is not on the approved list in `02-INTAKE.md`
- No number that would embarrass a client
- No claim about a result you cannot produce evidence for

Everything else is a judgement call. This one is not.

---

## 6. The weekly ritual

**Monday, 20 minutes.** Pick the week's four posts from the idea bank. Assign the
mix. If any slot is empty, pull a question from `09-QUESTION-BANK.md` and record a
two-minute voice answer.

**Every posting day, 15 minutes.** Draft, run THE GATE, attach media, schedule.

**Every posting day, 30 minutes after posting.** Reply to every comment with an
open-ended question, not a thank-you. A thank-you ends the thread. A question keeps
it alive, and the thread is what keeps the post being shown to people.

**Friday, 10 minutes.** Log what happened. Not likes — bookmarks, shares, DMs,
and any comment where someone tagged a colleague. Those are the signals that
matter (see `11-MEASUREMENT.md`).

**Monthly, 45 minutes.** Run the self-audit in `11-MEASUREMENT.md`. Export your
top 50, classify them, and change one thing about next month based on what you find.

**Quarterly.** Re-read `02-INTAKE.md` and update it. Every downstream output is
only as current as that file.

---

## 7. Concentrate the team, do not scatter it

When a post matters, **everyone comments on the original**. Do not have five people
each publish their own version of the same message — you get five weak signals
instead of one strong one, and you compete with yourself in the feed.

One post. Everyone in the comments. Different angles, real sentences, no "Great
post!" One strong signal beats five weak ones.

---

## 8. When to judge it

**Not before 90 days.** Nothing about a content account is readable earlier than
that. Reach is volatile, the audience is still forming, and the compounding has not
started.

At 90 days, ask three questions in this order:
1. Are the right *kinds* of people engaging? (Not how many — who.)
2. Are bookmarks, shares and tag-a-colleague comments trending up?
3. Has anyone mentioned a post on a sales call?

If the answer to all three is no, change the content. If the answer to 1 and 2 is
yes and 3 is no, keep going — 3 is the slowest of the three to arrive, and it is
the one that pays.
